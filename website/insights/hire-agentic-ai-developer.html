How to hire an agentic AI developer

Everyone says they "do agentic AI" now. Here's how to tell who can take an idea all the way to a system you trust — and who can only make a demo.

The hard part of hiring for agentic AI isn't finding people who can call an LLM. It's finding someone who understands that the model is the easy part, and that the value lives in everything around it: evaluation, integration, reliability, and judgment about what's worth building at all. Here's what to look for.

The skills that actually matter

  • Systems engineering, not just prompting. Anyone can prompt. You want someone who designs the loop around the model — tools, state, retries, fallbacks — like the software system it is.
  • Evaluation discipline. Ask how they'd measure whether an agent works. If the answer isn't "build a test set and score against it," keep looking.
  • Integration experience. Real value comes from connecting to your tools and data. Ask what they've actually wired agents into.
  • Judgment about scope. A strong hire will sometimes tell you not to build something, or to use an off-the-shelf tool. That honesty is worth more than enthusiasm.

Questions that separate signal from hype

  • "How would you know this agent is good enough to trust?" — listens for evals, not vibes.
  • "What happens when a tool call fails or the model goes off the rails?" — listens for guardrails and failure handling.
  • "Walk me through something you shipped that real users relied on." — separates demos from production.
  • "When would you tell a client not to build an agent?" — tests judgment and honesty.
  • "How do you keep cost predictable as it scales?" — tests production thinking.

Red flags

  • Talks only about models and frameworks, never about evaluation, data, or failure modes.
  • Quotes a price before understanding your workflows — or quotes suspiciously low (the reliability work is being skipped).
  • Can show a flashy demo but can't explain how they'd make it dependable.
  • No interest in how you'd own and maintain the system after handover.

Researcher vs. shipper — and why you want both

Pure researchers can push the frontier but may never ship something operable. Pure builders ship fast but can miss why an approach is fragile. The rare, valuable profile is someone with research depth and shipping discipline — who understands the models well enough to know their failure modes, and engineers around them so the result holds up in production.

How to structure the engagement

Start small and de-risked. A fixed-price audit tells you what's worth building and reveals how the person thinks before you commit to a full build. Then a fixed-scope build sprint so the estimation risk sits with them, not you. Avoid open-ended hourly arrangements with someone unproven.
