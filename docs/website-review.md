# XForward website review: recommended changes

Consultant review of the landing page (`website/index.html`, live at
https://ghazigh.github.io/ProjectX/), written from the point of view of
someone who has sold and delivered AI and agentic projects to small and
mid-sized businesses.

**Bottom line:** the page looks premium, but it sells to people who already
understand agentic AI. A typical owner of a 10–100 person business will leave
with three open questions: *Can I trust this person? What will it really cost
me, including after launch? What's my risk?* Most of the changes below
answer those three questions.

Status legend: `[ ]` to do · `[x]` done

---

## Priority 1: fix before sending the link to any prospect

- [ ] **1. Real domain and professional email.** A `github.io/ProjectX`
  address and a `@gmail.com` contact read as a side project. Buy `xforward.ca`
  (or `.com`), point GitHub Pages at it, and set up `ghazi@xforward.ca`
  (Google Workspace / Microsoft 365, about $8/month).
- [ ] **2. Add proof** (there is none: no clients, testimonials, demo or photo).
  - *Live demo:* "Try the booking agent: text (514) XXX-XXXX." The working
    agent is already in `lead_agent/`.
  - *90-second video:* the founder, on camera, walking through one agent
    doing real work.
  - *Founding-client program:* "3 founding spots: 30% off in exchange for
    a case study." Honest about having no track record, and a reason to act now.
- [ ] **3. Stop saying "we" if you work alone.** Say "I" in the founder
  section. Use "Senior AI engineer" or "A senior engineer + trusted partners".
  Being the only person is a selling point: "The person you meet is the
  person who builds it."
- [ ] **4. Back up the ROI promise.** Agree on one measurable target in the
  diagnostic (e.g. 20 h/week saved). "If the agent doesn't hit it within 30
  days of launch, I keep working at no extra cost." Put it in the Deployment
  card and the FAQ.
- [ ] **5. Show the running cost after launch.** Under each price: "Running
  costs (AI usage + hosting): typically $50–300/month, billed to your own
  accounts." Add a cheaper tier between nothing and the $4,000/month retainer:
  "Care plan, from $600/month: monitoring, fixes, small changes."

## Priority 2: raise conversion (first month)

- [ ] **6. Rewrite the hero around the result.** Keep "We send an engineer.
  Not a slide deck." Subline: "I find the work eating your team's hours and
  put AI agents on it. Live in 6 weeks, fixed price, measured in hours saved."
  Eyebrow: "AI agents for small businesses · Montréal".
- [ ] **7. A smaller first step than a sales call.** "Send me one task that
  eats your week. I'll reply within 48 h with whether an agent can do it, and
  roughly what it'd cost." It's a 3-field form. Also let people get their
  calculator result by email.
- [ ] **8. Make each use case something a client can buy.** Add "Typical
  project: $12–15k · 4–5 weeks · ~$150/month to run", "What you provide: …",
  and a "Get this for my business" button.
- [ ] **9. Pick a lead industry.** Lead with home services (the flagship agent
  exists). Later, give each industry its own page (e.g. `/home-services`) for Google.
- [ ] **10. Say what the client has to commit.** "One point person, about
  2 hours a week, and access to the tools involved."
- [ ] **11. Answer the questions clients actually ask in the FAQ.**
  - Which AI do you use, and is our data used to train it?
  - What if you disappear? (You own the code, accounts and documentation.)
  - Do we need to change our software?
  - What does it cost to run each month?
  - When is AI *not* a good fit?
- [ ] **12. Show more of the founder.** Real photo, why you do this, one
  concrete story from past work (anonymized).

## Priority 3: technical and legal basics

- [ ] **13. French for the Québec market.** Québec's French-language law
  (Bill 96) has rules for business websites aimed at Québec customers; check
  with a lawyer. Safe setup: French when the visitor's browser is in French,
  its own address (`/fr/`), English one click away.
- [ ] **14. Privacy policy and legal footer.** Québec's privacy law (Law 25)
  requires a published privacy policy once you collect personal information.
  Add `/privacy`, a named person responsible for privacy, and the NEQ
  (business registration number) in the footer.
- [ ] **15. SEO.** French text and the use cases are generated by JavaScript,
  so search engines see very little of them. Write the use cases into the
  HTML, make a separate French page, add a 1200×630 `og:image`, and add
  privacy-friendly analytics (e.g. Plausible).
- [ ] **16. Shorten the hero scroll.** Cut it from 340vh to about 200vh. Keep
  a "Book a call" button visible throughout, and show a small "skip" arrow.
- [ ] **17. Make the blog match.** The `/insights` articles still use the old
  design and header.

## Suggested order

| Week | Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | #1 domain + email, #3 "I" not "we", #5 running costs + care plan, #4 ROI promise, #6 hero subline |
| 2 | #2 live SMS demo, #12 photo + story, #7 "send me one task" form, #11 FAQ, #14 privacy page |
| 3–4 | #2 video, #8 per-use-case price + #10 time needed, #13 French-first, #15 SEO + og:image |
| Later | #9 industry pages, #17 blog redesign, turn early clients into case studies |

**What to leave alone:** the visual design, the dark mode, the use-case scenes,
the calculator and the fixed-price model are already better than most
competitors. The gaps are trust, clarity and risk, not looks.
